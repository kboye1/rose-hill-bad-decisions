# Rose Hill: Bad Decisions — After Hours, version 19

## What this version is trying to feel like

A tiny campus sandbox where expensive education, family connections, startup posturing and afterparty damage control collide. The joke is the gap between a polished maroon-and-navy personal brand and spectacularly unprofessional behavior. Characters are fictional adult students. Humor is profane and suggestive, without explicit sexual scenes.

The playable promise is concrete: wander the researched central campus, talk your way into stories, borrow the cart, complete quick jobs, start a dumb scuffle, escape security, read the resulting gossip, spend your payout, and keep roaming. None of the four stories is mandatory. This is an original small browser game inspired by open-world conventions; it does not use GTA assets or claim GTA V’s production scale, animation or realism.

## A connected evening

You begin with $60 and no required objective. Four short stories offer different kinds of college trouble. Four replayable quick jobs provide timers and records. Cash, trust, buzz, wanted stars and health connect these activities. A fight can make a story less profitable socially even if you recover its item; a quiet favor can unlock a later option. All of these choices happen in the same walkable campus scene.

### The Hostile Takeover

Elliot runs CUFFLINK, an allegedly exclusive networking startup with a suspicious user count. His rival Blaine has taken the deck before the Hughes alumni mixer.

1. Meet Elliot outside Hughes and accept or decline.
2. Find Blaine at the McShane pre-game.
3. Recover the deck through one of three routes: pay $40; challenge his pitch with a public verbal takedown; or win a non-graphic scuffle. A bad pitch answer allows retry or leaving. A failed fight does not erase the story.
4. Read the incriminating slide and return to Hughes.
5. Expose the fabricated numbers for $100, trust and a Maya callback, or sell the fantasy for $220, more buzz and less trust. The brawl route reduces the honest ending’s trust benefit, on top of the normal first-assault penalty.

The new story is optional and can be left at any stage. It uses actual movement, dialogue, cash deductions and combat state. Merely tracking a waypoint never advances it. Its reward can only pay once per evening.

### The existing three stories

- **The Walk of Shame:** Jules has someone else’s rugby shirt, one shoe and an urgent need for discretion. Find the shoe or bring the cart directly. Keeping it private earns trust and a presentation favor; gossip has different consequences.
- **The Presentation After the Party:** Maya and Nico turn a disastrous dating-app screenshot deck into either a rescued analysis or an embarrassing improvisation. The fictional course is AI and Data-Driven Marketplaces, taught by Professor Apostolos Filippas. Existing rehearsal and earned-favor branches remain.
- **The Ram Van Meetup:** Dev’s group cannot agree on a meeting point for what Preston insists is an alumni networking event. Find Sam and regroup or move the afterparty to Eddie’s. The game does not simulate a real shuttle schedule or a trip to Lincoln Center.

Resolved stories now create distinct phone messages. Characters have follow-up dialogue when approached again.

## Scuffles and consequences

X or left-click starts a punch. The first click captures the camera without attacking. Z or right mouse holds a guard. Q keeps the cup-throw action. Punching needs you on foot with the game running, enough stamina, a nearby target in front of you, and a clear line of sight.

Hits have a windup, alternating hand animation, short stun and modest knockback. Brave students pursue and retaliate after being provoked. Other students flee, and nearby bystanders may flee or film. Students do not randomly attack a player who has left them alone. Blocking reduces incoming damage substantially and spends stamina. Repeated hits produce a nine-second temporary knockdown; the opponent then gets up. No blood, death or permanent removal is simulated.

A first assault on a new person costs one trust. Each hit raises wanted heat; security officers are tougher and react more strongly. Punching people pays no chaos points or cash. Existing security pursuit, line-of-sight searching and fines remain active. A player defeat costs up to $20 and returns the player to Eddie’s with health restored. Security can charge up to $15 plus its existing chaos penalty. Neither event erases story progress.

Chill keeps short pursuits, forgiving driving and interactions, slower retaliation and reduced incoming damage. Stamina regenerates. Health regenerates after a quiet interval, and coffee provides a paid shortcut. The game is still intended to be easy to try on a class computer.

## RamPulse and the small economy

P opens a fictional phone that pauses the simulation. It contains story messages, a bounded campus feed, cash balance, a founder-story shortcut, a Starbucks waypoint and lighting controls. It does not access any real account, messages or payments.

An evening starts at $60. Most completed stories pay $30; the founder endings pay their stated amounts. Quick jobs pay approximately their chaos reward divided by 20. Coffee costs $8 and restores health/stamina without clearing wanted stars. Insufficient funds do not go negative, and the player can always recover health for free. Story pickups take interaction priority over coffee.

Cash, gossip and stories reset with a new evening. Appearance and job records retain their existing browser-local persistence.

## The business-prep and party aesthetic

The source-researched buildings, ram, fountain, courtyard, McShane and gym stay in place. New event dressing is explicitly fictional: a CUFFLINK founder mixer with laptops and signage outside Hughes, a student DJ table on Eddie’s, and a string of warm bulbs across the event area. Central-lawn benches and the event tables have simple collision, which also helps camera clearance.

Student outfits now follow finance, prep, afterparty and varsity archetypes rather than arbitrary combinations. Vests have quilt lines; blazers have buttons and a lapel pin; shoes, glasses, watches, phones, collars and skin/hair options are retained. Elliot and Blaine have dedicated roles. Punching, guarding, staggering, lying down and getting up use the existing articulated rig. First-person hands accompany the combat controls.

Default sunset uses warmer light and longer shadows. The phone can switch to daylight or a blue after-hours scene with illuminated glazing and local pools of warm light. This changes the actual 3D lighting. It does not change the simulation’s difficulty or imply a real campus event schedule.

## Verification and limits

Fourteen automated suites cover story continuity, all founder routes/endings, rewards, cash limits, coffee, phone pause, combat timing/range, retaliation, guard behavior, recovery, avatar variants, cameras, steps, boundaries and job systems. Browser QA verifies the rendered scene and a full roast/expose founder playthrough, along with punching, blocking, knockdown, security fines and phone/night controls.

The art remains procedural and stylized. There are no building interiors, licensed character scans, recorded dialogue, full vehicle traffic or online multiplayer. Architectural measurements and minor details retain the limitations documented in the campus research brief. These limits are stated so future work can focus on observable improvements rather than claiming features that are not implemented.
