# Campus exterior research

Research checked November 9, 2026. This is a reference brief for rebuilding McShane, Keating, Dealy and Hughes. No game code changed during this research pass.

The observations below come from inspecting exterior photographs, cross-checked with architect and university descriptions. They are visual observations, not surveyed dimensions. Construction dates and photo dates differ; do not treat every older photograph as a current-condition record. Establish each building's silhouette, facade orientation, massing and window rhythm before adding decorative detail.

## McShane Campus Center

Sources:

- [HLW, project architect](https://www.hlw.com/project/fordham-university-mcshane/)
- [Spark Studio, project lighting designer: exterior photographs by Frank Oudeman](https://www.sparksld.com/projects/fordham-university)
- [Fordham: featured spaces and connected buildings](https://www.fordham.edu/about/campuses/rose-hill-campus/mcshane-campus-center/featured-spaces/)

Observed exterior: a modern, asymmetric composition. Tall, narrow groups of glazing alternate with dark rough-stone piers. A prominent rectangular glazed tower rises above the lower wing, with a broad pale-stone side wall carrying the university seal. Dark metal mullions run through the glass. The entrance sits beside the tower beneath a projecting canopy; glass arcade connections join the older buildings. The photographed approach is paved and has slender traditional lamps and planted edges.

Current model mismatch: the shared modern-building treatment does not reproduce the tower, facade rhythm, entrance composition or connected volumes.

Rebuild priorities: separate lower wing, tower, recessed entrance and glass connections; distinguish rough dark stone, smooth pale stone and reflective glazing. Use full-height vertical glazing rather than rows of interchangeable small windows. Check which elevation faces the lawn against the campus map before positioning the entrance.

## Keating Hall

Source: [Kouzmanoff Bainton, renovation architect; frontal exterior photograph](https://kblarchitects.com/Fordham-Keating-Hall).

Observed exterior: a tall, slender central square tower above broad, much lower wings. Its large pointed traceried opening faces Edwards Parade. Projecting corner turrets, horizontal stone bands, ornamental parapets and narrow vertical details define the tower. The projecting entrance/chapel bay has a deep pointed entrance below a large Gothic window and decorative stone panel. Wing windows differ by level: Gothic grouped openings below, rectangular windows above. The masonry is mottled brown-gray with pale trim, rather than uniformly green-gray. A raised terrace and broad steps connect it to the lawn; large mature trees partly obscure the wings.

Current model mismatch: the tower is too squat and wide, with an oversized sideways arch, repetitive small windows and blocky battlements. The entrance and facade are too flat. Widening the entire scene did not fix the tower's vertical proportions.

Rebuild priorities: correct tower-to-wing height and width ratios using the frontal photograph; model the tall pointed opening and corner turrets; give the central bay depth, recessed doorway and correct window hierarchy. Avoid copying the wing facade texture onto the tower.

## Dealy Hall

Sources:

- [Kouzmanoff Bainton, renovation architect: exterior roof photograph and wing description](https://kblarchitects.com/Fordham-Dealy-Hall)
- [Ground-level side photograph, author-uploaded reference on Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dealy_Hall_side.jpg)
- [Raymond Bucko, S.J., Dealy and fountain photograph, taken March 21, 2014](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fordham_Dealy_Hall_and_fountain.jpg)

Observed exterior: substantial multi-level stone wings, not a low two-story hall. The ground-level side photograph shows a rectangular-window base, a round-arched window tier, a tier with shallow curved window heads, paired narrow round-headed upper windows, and a top band of rectangular windows. Pale corner quoins, lintels and horizontal bands contrast with the darker masonry. The roofline is flat, with railings and rooftop equipment/screens visible in the architect's photograph. The fountain photograph shows how wings frame a planted paved court, though its 2014 date limits conclusions about current landscaping.

Current model mismatch: the generic Gothic facade, low height, repeated pointed windows and pitched roof lose Dealy's identity. A single rectangular footprint also needs checking against its separate wings.

Rebuild priorities: wing composition and height first; flat roof and top window band; distinct window patterns by floor; pale quoins and masonry bands. Verify the lawn-facing facade separately from the courtyard and rear elevations.

## Hughes Hall

Sources:

- [HLW, renovation architect: exterior photographs](https://www.hlw.com/project/fordham-university-gabelli-school-of-business/)
- [Fordham: Architecture Brings Digital Age to Hughes Hall, October 15, 2013](https://now.fordham.edu/inside-fordham/architecture-brings-digital-age-to-hughes-hall/)
- [Fordham: Hughes Hall and its terrace](https://www.fordham.edu/gabelli-school-of-business/academic-programs-and-admissions/undergraduate-programs/which-campus/hughes-hall/)

Observed exterior: a long gray-stone historic facade with bright pale trim and corner quoins. Window shapes change by level: rectangular base windows, round-headed windows above, shallow curved heads on the next tier, and paired narrow round-headed windows at the top of the masonry facade. A pronounced pale cornice separates the stonework from a sloped green/blue glass mansard-form roof with regular metal ribs. The entrance uses sliding glass doors, a transparent canopy, broad pale steps and handrails. The terrace includes planting, seating, the ram sculpture and nearby Cunniffe Fountain.

Current model mismatch: the flat roof is a major omission. The upper three window tiers should not all use identical arches. The canopy should read as glass, and the cornice, quoins and entry steps need more depth.

Rebuild priorities: add the glass roof silhouette; distinguish every window tier; articulate the cornice and corner trim; rebuild the glass entry and surrounding terrace from photographs. Check fountain, sculpture and terrace placement against multiple views instead of placing them by guesswork.

## Modeling sequence

1. Confirm facade orientation and relative footprint on the official campus map.
2. Block out each building's separate volumes and roof/tower silhouette.
3. Compare frontal and oblique views with the photographs before applying textures.
4. Add building-specific window tiers, entrances and material contrasts.
5. Add landscaping after the building positions and dimensions are stable.

Remaining uncertainty: exact dimensions, hidden elevations, current small landscape details and full facade window counts are not established. These require additional views or plans; do not invent them and describe the result as exact.

Reference photographs remain on their source sites and are not bundled as game assets. Retain photographer credit and license information if any photograph is later distributed.

## Hughes implementation note — version 16

Rechecked November 10, 2026. [HLW’s completed exterior gallery](https://www.hlw.com/project/fordham-university-gabelli-school-of-business/) shows gray granite, pale quoins, four different stone-facade window tiers and the green glass mansard roof. The entrance close-up shows thin HUGHES HALL letters over a projecting green glass canopy, sliding glass doors, stainless brackets and handrails. The oblique twilight photograph establishes the roof slope and broad low stair flight. [Fordham’s renovation account](https://now.fordham.edu/university-news/architecture-brings-digital-age-to-hughes-hall-video-2/) describes the wide Vermont marble entrance steps. [Fordham’s Hughes page](https://www.fordham.edu/gabelli-school-of-business/academic-programs-and-admissions/undergraduate-programs/which-campus/hughes-hall/) confirms the ram sculpture and nearby fountain/terrace.

The dedicated model replaces the old flat-roof facade and almost-flat decorative steps. It adds window tiers, quoins, cornice, a ribbed glass roof, transparent canopy and a 1.02m landing with six 0.17m walkable treads. These dimensions, the count of bays/treads, rear elevations, ram sculpture and planting are game approximations, not measured claims. Procedural materials and original decorative glazing artwork are distributed; reference photographs remain on their source sites.

## Version 21 exterior detail

The November 10, 2026 review focused on visible details from the lawn and pedestrian approaches. Map distances and details remain approximate.

- **Keating and Eddie’s.** The frontal photograph shows projecting rounded corner turrets and pale courses, a pointed tower opening, upper rectangular wing windows, and mature trees framing the entrance. The model adds layered stone mouldings, set-back glazing, a shaped tower crown and varied brown-gray masonry. The maroon banner sits between the portal and upper window. [Keating renovation architect](https://kblarchitects.com/Fordham-Keating-Hall) · [Fordham’s August 2026 grounds article](https://now.fordham.edu/inside-fordham/getting-campus-ready-the-work-behind-the-welcome/)

The courtyard fountain has two bowls, an open circular basin and animated water. [Hughes project architect](https://www.hlw.com/project/fordham-university-gabelli-school-of-business/) · [Fordham’s Hughes Hall overview](https://www.fordham.edu/gabelli-school-of-business/academic-programs-and-admissions/undergraduate-programs/which-campus/hughes-hall/)

The gym uses rougher fieldstone and climbing ivy around the outer facade. [McShane entrance photos](https://www.fordham.edu/about/campuses/rose-hill-campus/mcshane-campus-center/) · [Fordham Athletics’ April 2026 gym exterior](https://fordhamsports.com/news/2026/4/1/womens-basketball-neil-harrow-named-head-womens-basketball-coach-at-fordham.aspx)
